# GEO and structured data — 2026 findings

Researched September 2026. This area moves; re-check before relying on it in a
year. Sources at the bottom.

## Two common beliefs that are wrong

**llms.txt does not drive AI citations.** As of 2026 it is not consumed at
inference time by ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity or Copilot in any
documented production system. It describes navigation; it is not access control
and not a ranking input. Publishing one is harmless, but if the goal is being
cited, on-page structured data moves the needle considerably more.

**robots.txt is the real access lever, and the bots split by purpose.** The
split matters because it lets a site refuse training without losing visibility:

| Bot | Purpose | Blocking it costs |
|---|---|---|
| GPTBot | OpenAI training | nothing in search |
| OAI-SearchBot | ChatGPT search index | ChatGPT citations |
| Claude-SearchBot | Claude search | Claude citations |
| PerplexityBot | Perplexity index | Perplexity citations |

Allow the search bots. AI referrals are high-intent traffic.

## What makes a passage get quoted

Princeton's GEO research found the strongest techniques lift AI visibility by
roughly 30–40% over unoptimised content, and they are unglamorous:

- Cite sources, and name them.
- Include concrete statistics rather than adjectives.
- Quote people directly and attribute the quote.
- Use authoritative, specific language.

Structurally, phrase H2 and H3 headings as the question a reader would actually
type. Answer engines are matching prompts; a heading that is already a question
in the user's own words is a short path to being the passage that answers it.

Name the entity explicitly rather than relying on pronouns. A model extracting
facts from a page needs "שני ששון" and "הוד השרון" to appear as words, not as
"we" and "here".

## Structured data

JSON-LD only. Google recommends it and both Microdata and RDFa are error-prone
and harder to keep in sync with the page.

Highest value types for a local service business, for both local search and AI
citation: `LocalBusiness`, `Service`, and `Person` / `Organization`.

**The self-serving review rule.** Since 2019 Google has not shown review stars
for reviews a business publishes about itself via `LocalBusiness` or
`Organization` markup. Still true in 2026. Reviews attached to a specific
`Service` or `Product` the business offers remain eligible for review rich
results.

So: marking up business-level reviews is fine and still feeds AI answers, but it
will not produce stars in Google. Do not tell the user it will. If stars are the
goal, the reviews have to attach to a service or product — and only if they
genuinely review that specific thing.

## Local ranking weights

Roughly, for the local pack:

| Signal | Share |
|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | ~32% |
| Review signals | ~16% |
| Citation consistency | ~7% |

Within the Business Profile, the heaviest individual factors are the primary
category, the proximity of the listed address to the searcher, and keywords in
the business title.

Businesses with consistent name, address and phone across directories are
reported around 40% more likely to appear in the local pack. That consistency
now also feeds how AI engines decide a business identity is real, which is why
the propagation checklist in `brand-facts.md` is worth actually working through
rather than filing away.

Core citation set: Google, Apple, Bing, Yelp, plus the local directories — in
Israel that means d.co.il, easy.co.il, t.co.il, Waze and mit4mit.
